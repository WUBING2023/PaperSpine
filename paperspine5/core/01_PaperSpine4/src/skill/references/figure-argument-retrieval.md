# Retrieve a figure's argument, then transfer its evidence structure

Use when a new or substantially redesigned figure lacks a convincing contribution,
matched references or connected panel logic. For a local correction, reuse a valid
mapping. This extends question/story planning; do not repeat the same plan in new
forms. Keep the useful findings in the existing figure brief. It does not authorize
new analyses or broaden the saved research mode.

## Turn the claim into a discriminating evidence question

State the specific claim using the study's actual objects, outcome and comparison.
Separate a proposed claim from a verified finding; a supplied discussion's SOTA or
mechanism assertion is context until its evaluation and evidence are checked.

Abstract the *argument*, preserving its inferential difficulty. For example,
"an auxiliary conservation channel improves binding prediction" becomes "an
auxiliary prior contributes information beyond the retained inputs." It does not
become "the biological process is causal." Identify the strongest plausible
alternative that available evidence could distinguish: extra model capacity,
different training conditions, a group-level proxy, input distribution shift,
selection of favorable examples, or another study-specific explanation.

Ask the smaller question that would distinguish these accounts, then specify the
needed observation/control and a result that would weaken the proposed answer.
For a spatial prior, informative questions might be whether its location matters
beyond its overall level, or whether gains persist under comparable model training.
Inference-time corruption and a retrained ablation answer different questions;
neither automatically excludes a distribution-shift explanation or establishes a
biological mechanism. Choose applicable questions, not every listed example.

## Search for the comparison, not just the research topic

Search public sources with the evidence question's intervention, contrast or
validation terms plus a relevant model/data context. An initial query might pair
"auxiliary biological prior" with "matched ablation"; another might pair
"positional information" with "shift control" or "permutation control".
Translate the idea into the field's actual terminology after reading candidates.
Do not assume authors use the phrase "evidence question" or search only for a
chart type. A similar topic identifies candidates, not an argument match.

When same-field papers do not contain the needed contrast, widen to a neighboring
domain while keeping the evidence question fixed. A comparison involving a
different biological prior can transfer; its entities and conclusions cannot.
Keep sources that supply the decisive comparison or a complementary evidence
layer. Stop expanding when the useful structure and its limitations are understood;
do not collect a fixed count or use venue prestige as a relevance score. Preserve
applicable saved whole-paper learning requirements separately.

## Verify the argument inside the paper

For a retained exemplar, resolve the exact figure/panel through the publisher's
figure-caption binding or the PDF. Read the complete caption and the Results
passage that invokes this panel; inspect the actual image at readable scale.
Check what is held constant, what changes, the measured unit, statistical unit,
uncertainty, selection rule and the authors' bounded conclusion. Inspect neighboring
panels when the claimed comparison depends on them. Record a direct source and
figure/panel locator, plus the relevant Results subsection or passage locator.

Distinguish what the source visibly demonstrates from the source's interpretation
and the host's proposed transfer. An abstract, search snippet or caption alone
cannot verify panel linkage. If text or image access is missing, retain that
candidate as unverified and use a viewable source or an explicitly original design;
do not invent the missing comparison. A correct figure number with the wrong image
is still an invalid exemplar.

## Convert the retrieved comparison into a drawing decision

Map each retained reference feature to current evidence before drawing:

- **Transfer:** the inferential job and why that comparison discriminates between
  the proposed answer and its alternative; name the actual region to change.
- **Current support:** exact result/source, object identifiers, fields, units,
  conditions and statistical level. Note a missing control or incompatible unit.
- **Visible linkage:** identify which shared objects, coordinate, comparison
  conditions or statistical level connects the panels. Carry that identity visibly
  through alignment, consistent marks, selected-object labels or a grounded inset.
- **Boundary:** what does not transfer, what remains unresolved and which wording
  the resulting figure can support.

Shared colors, a connecting arrow and a common title do not establish linkage.
A selected locus and a population summary need an explicit selection rule and
population membership; a task-level result and an RBP-level estimate need their
aggregation made clear. Do not imply pointwise matching through aligned panels
unless that matching exists. Give the decisive evidence spatial priority; place
supporting controls where readers can compare their relevant conditions directly.
Panel jobs, data bindings and caption details remain governed by the existing story
and mapping methods; record these additions there rather than creating a new ledger.

When the current evidence cannot instantiate a reference's decisive comparison,
choose a narrower supported question, another structure, or the specific missing
experiment. Perform that experiment only under existing authorization. Mark a
proposed experiment as unperformed. Adding more chart families, heatmap cells,
labels or decorative connectors cannot supply the missing evidence.

Optional brief fragment, reusable in prose or a small table:

> Question / strongest alternative / discriminating comparison / exact viewed
> reference panel and Results context / current evidence or gap / visible panel
> linkage / conclusion and a result that would weaken it.

## Test the delivered figure as an argument

Inspect the rendered candidate alongside the current figure and the relevant
published exemplar. Locate the decisive comparison without relying on its title
to state the answer. Check that linked panels preserve object/condition identity,
that controls address the stated alternative, and that uncertainty and contrary
cases remain visible. Ask which plausible result would change the conclusion and
whether the encoding would reveal it rather than conceal it by sorting, clipping,
normalization or selective examples. A reviewer must be able to identify the
specific supported contribution and the remaining inferential boundary.

Record the largest remaining gap as evidence or design: the former requires a
narrower claim or identified experiment; the latter requires a source-level change
and another visual inspection. A changed palette, extra panel or higher subjective
score is not evidence that the argument improved. Deliver the actual candidate,
editable source, comparison and concise changed decision; do not mark reference
learning complete solely because this note was filled.

## Teaching sources and attribution

The retrieval procedure above is this skill's operational synthesis, not a method
claimed by these sources. These complementary resources inform figure craft:

- [MIT Communication Lab, Figure Design](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/aeroastro/commkit/figure-design/): message and evidence selection.
- [Bang Wong, Layout](https://www.nature.com/articles/nmeth.1711): hierarchy and spatial organization.
- [Nature Methods, Points of review (part 1)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nmeth0211-101): visual relationships and figure revision.
- [Rougier et al., Ten Simple Rules for Better Figures](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/doi?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1003833): audience, message and informative design.
- [Rougier, Scientific Visualization: Python + Matplotlib](https://github.com/rougier/scientific-visualization-book): practical typography, layout, color and reproducible drawing.
