# PaperSpine Research

Read the user's research scope and identify their actual materials, question, intended audience and limits. Keep original inputs in place. For a paper based on supplied results, inspect the methods, figures and comparisons that bear on the claims. Map each main finding to its actual source. Use [research and supplied evidence](../../skill/references/research.md) for this distinction. Treat research scope and author decisions expressed in chat or saved for this paper the same way you would treat confirmed workbench inputs.

For a new study, follow [the full research method](../../skill/references/research-full-cycle.md). Read the closest primary work and connect a genuine gap to a feasible design. Before a run, fix the question, comparator and units, endpoint, analysis, and relevant train/evaluation boundaries. Pilot with authorized data and tools. Execute the study within the requested budget, or report the actual dependency preventing it. Keep producing scripts, settings, relevant failures and result files. Explain effects and uncertainty at the appropriate unit. An exploratory or negative result can support a paper with a calibrated claim.

Offer evidence-backed motivation or contribution options when the choice would change the paper. Provide the source-linked claims, methods, outputs and important limitations at the level needed for writing or figures. A literature-based request can end with a literature synthesis and a proposed design when empirical evidence is unavailable. For venue learning and citation support, use [journal learning](../../skill/references/journal-learning.md) and [citation checks](../../skill/references/citation.md).

For direct use with a managed installation, see [updates](../EDITION-MANAGEMENT.md) if available. In a full run, use the orchestrator's existing check.
